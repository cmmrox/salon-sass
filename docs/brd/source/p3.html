8. Functional requirements

Requirements are grouped by module. Identifiers are stable and should be carried into the backlog. The priority, release and prototype markers are explained in “How to read the requirement tables”.

8.2 Identity, roles and access

8.5 Booking engine

BKG-01 | Availability is calculated in real time from opening hours, each professional’s hours and breaks, leave, blocked time, existing bookings, current holds and service buffers. | M | R1 | Y BKG-02 | The salon sets the slot interval, the minimum notice before an appointment, and how far ahead customers can book. | M | R1 | P BKG-03 | “Any available professional” assigns an eligible professional by a strategy the salon chooses. | M | R1 | Y BKG-04 | Selecting a time places a hold (10 minutes by default) with a visible countdown. The hold appears on the staff calendar, is not released while a payment is in progress, and is released automatically on expiry. | M | R1 | Y BKG-05 | Two bookings or holds can never overlap for the same professional. This is guaranteed by the database under simultaneous requests, not only checked by the application. | M | R1 | P BKG-06 | Booking states: held, pending payment, confirmed, arrived, in service, completed, cancelled, no-show. Each change records who made it, when, and why. | M | R1 | Y BKG-07 | A customer can reschedule within the salon’s policy. Availability is checked again at confirmation, and any payment carries over. | M | R1 | Y BKG-08 | A customer can cancel. The policy and the exact refund are shown before they confirm. | M | R1 | Y BKG-09 | Policies are set per salon: deposit (percentage or amount, cap, per-service override), free-cancellation window, amount retained on late cancellation or no-show, limits on rescheduling. | M | R1 | P BKG-10 | Walk-ins: check immediate availability, start now, book the next free time, or join a queue with an estimated wait. Confirmed bookings are never moved to make room. | M | R1 | Y BKG-11 | Staff can create a booking on a customer’s behalf, recording the source (phone, WhatsApp, in person). | M | R1 | Y BKG-12 | Calendar views: day timeline by professional, list, and week with occupancy. Bookings can be dragged to a new time or professional with validation and a stated reason when a move is refused. The customer is notified of any change. | M | R1 | Y BKG-13 | An attention list shows bookings affected by leave, closures or removed services until staff resolve each one. | M | R1 | Y BKG-14 | Booking references are short, readable and unique within the salon. | M | R1 | Y BKG-15 | All times are stored in a universal form and shown in the salon’s time zone. | M | R1 | N BKG-16 | Every booking records its channel: app, website, shared link, Instagram, Google, WhatsApp, phone, walk-in. | M | R1 | P BKG-17 | Waitlist: a customer joins for a date, time band and professional; staff can notify them when a time opens; the customer must confirm. | S | R1 | Y BKG-18 | Waitlist offers are sent automatically when a matching time frees up, are valid for a limited time, and pass to the next person if unclaimed. | S | R2 | N BKG-19 | When no single professional can perform all chosen services, the engine offers consecutive appointments with different professionals as one booking. | S | R2 | P BKG-20 | Customer risk rules: after a set number of no-shows, full prepayment is required, or online booking is blocked. | S | R2 | N BKG-21 | Recurring appointments (for example every four weeks). | C | R3 | N BKG-22 | Group bookings (for example a bridal party) across several professionals. | C | R3 | N BKG-23 | Services at the customer’s location with travel time and an address. | C | R3 | N

8.7 Payments, checkout and refunds

The salon chooses which options are offered, overall and per service. | M | R1 | Y PAY-02 | Online payments go through the salon’s own gateway merchant account. 9. Non-functional requirements

NFR-01 | Tenant isolation | No request can read or change another tenant’s data, including through search, reports, exports, files and caches. | Enforced per section 7.2; automated cross-tenant tests on every build NFR-02 | Availability | Booking, calendar and checkout functions are available during trading hours and beyond. | 99.9% a month; planned maintenance outside 07:00 to 22:00 local time NFR-03 | Performance | Screens respond quickly on mid-range Android phones and mobile data. | Availability search under 500 ms at the 95th percentile; other reads under 300 ms; app cold start under 3 s NFR-04 | Capacity | The design supports growth without re-architecture. | Planning assumption: 2,000 tenants, 20 staff each at the top end, 5 million bookings a year NFR-05 | Concurrency | Simultaneous attempts on one slot produce exactly one booking. | Verified by load test before each major release NFR-06 | Recovery | Data can be restored after loss or error, for the platform or for one tenant. | Recovery point 15 minutes or better; recovery time 4 hours; restore tested quarterly NFR-07 | Security | Encryption in transit and at rest; secrets and gateway credentials encrypted with managed keys; least-privilege access; dependency and image scanning. | OWASP ASVS level 2 as the reference; independent penetration test before public launch and yearly NFR-08 | Authentication | Strong password policy and two-step verification for staff; rate limiting on sign-in and one-time codes. | Per IAM-06, IAM-10 NFR-09 | Privacy | Personal data is collected for stated purposes, with consent recorded, retention periods defined, and deletion and export supported. | Per section 10 NFR-10 | Auditability | Sensitive actions are logged immutably with actor, time, tenant and before-and-after values. | Retained 24 months NFR-11 | Payments integrity | No card data stored or processed by the platform. Every payment event is idempotent and traceable to a gateway reference. | Hosted checkout or gateway SDK only NFR-12 | Accessibility | Customer surfaces and workspace meet recognised accessibility criteria. | WCAG 2.2 level AA as the reference NFR-13 | Localisation | Languages, currency, number and date formats, time zone, tax labels and phone formats are configuration, not code. | English, Sinhala, Tamil; LKR default NFR-14 | Compatibility | The app supports current and recent operating-system versions and small Android devices. | Android 8 and later; iOS 16 and later (confirm against usage data); app download under 40 MB NFR-15 | Resilience | A failure of a messaging or payment provider degrades one function, not the system; work is retried. | Outbox with retry; timeouts and circuit breakers on every external call NFR-16 | Observability | Every log line, trace and error carries the tenant, and operators can see health per tenant. | Alerting on error rate, payment callback failures, message failures, job delays NFR-17 | Maintainability | No per-tenant code or branches. Tenant differences are configuration and entitlements. | Enforced by code review and architecture tests NFR-18 | Releasability | Backend and web are deployable at any time without downtime; older app versions keep working. | Backward-compatible API and migrations; minimum-version control NFR-19 | Data portability | A salon can export its customers, bookings, services and payments in an open format at any time. | CSV or spreadsheet export NFR-20 | Supportability | Support staff can reproduce what a salon or customer sees without asking for passwords. | Per PLT-07
