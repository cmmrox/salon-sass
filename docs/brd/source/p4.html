10.2 Who is responsible for personal data

  • For customer data (names, numbers, visit history, notes), the salon decides the purposes and the platform processes it on the salon’s behalf. The contract with each salon must include data-processing terms that say so.
  • For salon account, staff sign-in and billing data, the platform is responsible in its own right.
  • A salon’s customer list is that salon’s asset. The platform does not use it to market other salons, and does not combine customers across salons (IAM-03). This is both a privacy position and the commercial promise in section 5.6.

10.3 Sri Lanka’s Personal Data Protection Act

Status at the time of writing

The Personal Data Protection Act, No. 9 of 2022 was amended by Act No. 22 of 2025, passed in October 2025. The Data Protection Authority is established. The amendment removed the fixed commencement timetable, and sources from mid-2026 report that the core obligations on controllers and processors were still awaiting a commencement order. The position may have changed; legal counsel should confirm it before launch.

The prudent course is to build for the Act now, since the obligations are known and are far cheaper to design in than to retrofit:

Lawful basis and consent | Consent captured and recorded for marketing (CRM-04); transactional messages separated from marketing (NOT-06). Purpose limitation and minimisation | Only name and mobile number are mandatory to book. Birthday, email and notes are optional. Rights of the individual | Access, correction, deletion and export from the app and on request through the salon (IAM-05). Security | Section 9: isolation, encryption, access control, audit. Retention | Defined periods per data type; automatic anonymisation of inactive customers after the period the salon sets. Breach handling | Detection, an internal runbook, and the means to identify affected tenants and individuals quickly. Processor terms | Data-processing agreement with every salon; list of sub-processors (hosting, messaging, gateway). Cross-border transfer | Hosting outside Sri Lanka is to be assessed against the Act’s transfer rules (D-08). Sensitive data | Allergy and health notes in consultation forms (R2) need explicit consent and tighter role access.

11. Commercial model

This section is a hypothesis to test with pilot salons, not a price list.

11.1 Reference points

Fresha in Sri Lanka | LKR 1,190 per team member a month; a five-person salon is about LKR 5,950 a month before add-ons. Loyalty add-on LKR 6,050 per location a month. | The price a salon owner will compare against. The add-ons show where the perceived extras are. Booksy (US) | About US$29.99 a month plus US$20 per additional staff member | Per-staff pricing is the norm, and is disliked by growing salons. SaloBook (Sri Lanka) | Free in early access; states there will be no per-staff pricing | A local entrant is positioning on flat pricing. 11.2 Proposed packaging

Essentials | Independents and small salons | Branded booking site on a platform subdomain, calendar, walk-ins, customers, online payments, reports, push and email notifications | Branded space in the shared app Professional | Established salons | Everything in Essentials, plus a WhatsApp and SMS allowance, own domain, reviews, loyalty, campaigns, Sinhala and Tamil, more staff seats | Branded space in the shared app Signature | Salons that want their own app | Everything in Professional, plus dedicated iOS and Android apps in the salon’s name, multiple branches, priority support | Dedicated store apps
  • Charge basis. Flat monthly price per location in staff bands (for example up to 3, up to 10, more than 10), not per head. It is simpler to explain and removes a known irritant.
  • One-time fee for dedicated app set-up, covering enrolment support, listing preparation and first submission.
  • Pass-through of store fees (paid by the salon to Apple and Google) and of messages beyond the allowance, with the price per message shown before sending.
  • No commission on bookings or new customers, stated prominently.

13. Risks, assumptions and constraints

13.1 Risks

R-01 | Store rejection or account action because apps are treated as template spam | High if published from one account | High | Publish dedicated apps only in salons’ own accounts; unique listings; container app as the default. Section 7.4. R-02 | Salons cannot or will not complete developer enrolment (legal entity, D-U-N-S, yearly fee) | High | Medium | Booking site and shared app need no enrolment; guided enrolment service; dedicated app sold only where the salon qualifies. R-03 | Customers do not install a single-salon app | Medium | Medium | Booking site as an equal channel; give the app reasons to exist (rebook, loyalty, push); in-salon QR prompts. Track install and use per salon. R-04 | Operating many dedicated apps overwhelms a small team | Medium | High | Full automation of build and submit; over-the-air updates; registry and status dashboard; price the tier to fund it. R-05 | Data from one salon is exposed to another | Low | Critical | Section 7.2 guardrails; automated isolation tests; penetration test; logged operator access. R-06 | Price pressure from Fresha and free local entrants | High | Medium | Compete on brand ownership and local fit, not price; flat pricing; no commission. R-07 | Messaging costs exceed what is recovered | Medium | Medium | Push first; allowances; cost visible to the salon; abuse controls. R-08 | Double booking or wrong availability damages trust early | Medium | High | Database-level constraint; load tests; attention list; pilot before wider release. R-09 | Payment state errors: charged but not booked, or the reverse | Medium | High | Server-verified callbacks; idempotency; reconciliation job; clear pending state. R-10 | Gateway limitations (manual refunds for some methods, settlement delay, onboarding time for salons) | Medium | Medium | Provider abstraction; manual-refund workflow; pay-at-salon as a fallback from day one. R-13 | Poor connectivity in salons | Medium | Medium | Lightweight screens; tolerant states; today’s schedule cached for reading. R-14 | Imported data is poor (duplicates, wrong numbers) | Medium | Low | Import preview and validation; merge tool. R-15 | A store or provider policy changes | Medium | Medium | Review policies each quarter; keep the booking site independent of the stores.

13.2 Assumptions

A-01 | The launch market is Sri Lanka, with rupee pricing and the Asia/Colombo time zone as defaults. A-02 | The operator is, or will be, a registered legal entity able to hold organisation developer accounts, a messaging account and contracts with salons. A-03 | Salons that take online payments will open their own gateway merchant account. A-04 | The prototype represents the intended look, tone and flow, and is the starting point for design. A-05 | One backend, one database and one codebase per client type are fixed constraints, as stated by the product owner. A-06 | Technology choices are fixed as stated: React Native, Spring Boot on the latest stable release with the latest long-term-support Java and virtual threads, Next.js, PostgreSQL. A-07 | First-year scale is in the low hundreds of salons; the design target is 2,000. A-08 | Staff use the workspace in a browser on a phone, tablet or computer; no staff app is needed for the first release. A-09 | English is acceptable for pilot salons’ staff and customers at launch.

13.3 Constraints

  • App store policies, in particular Apple 4.2.6, are outside the operator’s control and determine the distribution model.
  • Local payment options are narrower than in markets served by global processors; split payments and platform-managed payouts are not assumed.
  • Per-message charges make messaging a variable cost that must be priced.
  • Small team: every design choice that adds per-tenant manual work is a cost that grows with success.

Appendix B. Glossary

Tenant | One salon business on the platform, with its own data, settings, branding and users. Multi-tenant | One running application and database serving many tenants, each isolated from the others. White-label | A product built once and presented under each customer’s brand. Row-level security | A PostgreSQL feature that restricts which rows a database session can see or change, according to a policy. Hold | A short reservation of a time slot while the customer completes a booking. Buffer | Time added after a service for clean-up or preparation; part of the appointment for scheduling, not shown to the customer as service time. Walk-in | A customer who arrives without an appointment. No-show | A customer who does not arrive for a confirmed appointment and did not cancel. One-time code (OTP) | A short code sent to a phone to prove the person controls that number. Container app | One store app that serves many businesses, each seen only by its own customers. Apple calls this the “picker” model. Over-the-air update | An update to an app’s JavaScript delivered directly, without a new store release. D-U-N-S number | A business identifier issued by Dun & Bradstreet, required by Apple and Google for organisation developer accounts. LANKAQR | Sri Lanka’s national QR code standard for payments. Merchant account | The salon’s account with a payment gateway, into which customer payments settle. Modular monolith | One deployable application divided internally into modules with enforced boundaries. MoSCoW | Prioritisation as Must, Should, Could (and Won’t for now). PDPA | Sri Lanka’s Personal Data Protection Act, No. 9 of 2022, as amended.
